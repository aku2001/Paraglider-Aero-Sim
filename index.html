Understanding the Parameters

Input Definitions

Projected Span & Root Chord The dimensions of the wing as it appears when fully inflated and flying (like the shadow it casts on the ground). This is different from the "flat" dimensions of the unrolled fabric.
Taper Ratio The ratio of the wingtip chord to the center (root) chord. Modern paragliders are tapered (elliptical) to reduce induced drag at the wingtips. A ratio of 1.0 is a perfect rectangle; 0.5 means the tips are half as wide as the center.
Trim Angle of Attack (AoA) The pitch angle of the wing relative to the oncoming air. In flight, you adjust this using the speed bar (which pitches the nose down, lowering AoA) or by releasing the trimmers.
Brake Applied Pulling the brake toggles curls the trailing edge of the wing downwards. This massively increases aerodynamic drag and forces the nose up, increasing your effective Angle of Attack until you eventually stall.
Altitude & Temperature These define the atmosphere. Higher altitudes and hotter temperatures result in thinner, less dense air. The glider must fly faster through thin air to generate the required lift to support your weight.
Pilot Mass & Frontal Area Total suspended weight (you + harness) dictates how fast gravity pulls you through the air. The frontal area represents your aerodynamic footprint. A bulky seated harness (high area) creates much more parasitic drag than a sleek, aerodynamic pod harness.

Telemetry Outputs

Tapered Area & Aspect Ratio The mathematical lifting surface of the glider. Aspect Ratio (Span squared divided by Area) describes how "long and skinny" the wing is. High aspect ratios glide much better but are more demanding to fly.
Air Density ($\rho$) Calculated from your altitude and temperature inputs using standard atmospheric physics. Measured in kilograms per cubic meter.
Effective AoA Your true Angle of Attack. If you pull the brakes, this number automatically increases past your baseline trim, indicating how dangerously close to the critical stall angle (usually around 12°-15°) you are.
Airspeed The true velocity of the paraglider moving through the air mass along its downward flight path.
Glide Ratio (L/D) The ratio of Lift to Drag. A glide ratio of 8.5:1 means that in zero wind, you will glide 8.5 meters forward for every 1 meter of altitude you lose.
Sink Rate Your vertical velocity towards the ground. Paraglider pilots strive to keep this number as low as possible to stay in the air longer.
